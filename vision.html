Computer Vision

Computer vision is about getting a program to understand pictures. Members train models that tell digits, clothes, or hand gestures apart, then test them on photos the model has never seen to find where it breaks.

Express interest What you'll build
About this animation

A 3 by 3 convolution sliding over real handwritten digits from MNIST. Each position of the kernel produces one pixel of the output on the right. Hover over the input to move the kernel yourself. The kernel changes after every pass.

What you'll learn and build

  • An image classifier trained in your browser in one meeting
  • A convolutional neural network in Keras that reads handwritten digits
  • A model trained on photos you take and label yourself
  • A webcam demo that runs your model live

Experience needed

A little Python. If you've used a notebook in the fall sessions, you're ready.

This year

Planned for 2026-27
  • A group project on photos that members take and label
  • Transfer learning: reusing a large pretrained model on a small dataset
  • A short report on where our best model fails, and why

Learning resources

  • Teachable MachineInteractive, 30 minutes

    Train an image, pose, or sound classifier from your webcam. No code.

  • CNN ExplainerInteractive, 30 minutes

    See what each layer of a convolutional network does to an image.

  • Kaggle Learn: Computer VisionCourse, 4 hours

    Convolutions, pooling, data augmentation, and transfer learning in Keras.

  • 3Blue1Brown: Neural NetworksVideo, 1 hour

    Watch the first four videos: what a neural network is, how it learns, and backpropagation. Start here.

  • TensorFlow PlaygroundInteractive, 30 minutes

    Train a tiny network in your browser and watch what each neuron learns.

  • Kaggle Learn: Intro to Deep LearningCourse, 4 hours

    Build and train networks in Keras, including dropout and batch normalization.

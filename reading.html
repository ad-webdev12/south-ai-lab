Reading group

A seven-session plan for the AI, Ethics and Society group.

Adapted from a university AI Safety Fundamentals (AISF) reading-group syllabus, shortened for a high school group. Maintained by the SAIL officers. Last reviewed September 2026. This field moves quickly, so check dates on anything you cite.

Required material takes about half an hour per session. In discussion, try to separate three things: what was measured, how someone interpreted it, and what they predict will happen next.

Session 1: Fairness and bias in systems that exist now

By the end you can: explain two ways a model can treat groups differently, and why fixing one can break another.

Required

  • AI Explorables: Measuring FairnessInteractive, about 20 minutes

    An interactive walk through why different fairness measures disagree.

  • Kaggle Learn: Identifying Bias in AICourse, lesson 3

    Six kinds of bias, with an exercise on a comment-moderation model.

Discuss: Pick one example from the reading. What was measured, and what was inferred from it?

Session 2: Where AI is headed

By the end you can: describe what is driving current progress, and separate measured trends from forecasts.

Required

  • Task-Completion Time HorizonsArticle, about 10 minutes

    METR measures how long a task AI systems can finish on their own, and how that number has changed.

  • Can AI scaling continue through 2030?Article, about 10 minutes

    Read the summary: what could limit progress, from power to chips to data.

Optional

  • Trends in Artificial IntelligenceInteractive, about 15 minutes

    Epoch AI's charts of the computing power, data, and money going into AI.

Discuss: Which claims in these readings are measurements, and which are predictions?

Session 3: When a system does what you said, not what you meant

By the end you can: explain the gap between a goal and the measurable stand-in used to train for it.

Required

  • Specification gamingArticle, about 15 minutes

    Google DeepMind's collection of AI systems that found loopholes in their instructions.

  • Aligning language models to follow instructionsArticle, about 15 minutes

    How OpenAI used human feedback to make GPT-3 follow instructions.

Optional

  • Playing the training gameArticle, about 20 minutes

    An argument that training on human approval can reward looking good over being good.

Discuss: Find an example from school or sports where people optimized the measurement. How is the AI case similar?

Session 4: When a model learns the wrong goal

By the end you can: describe how a model can pass its tests and still behave differently later.

Required

  • The other AI alignment problem: mesa-optimizersVideo, about 23 minutes

    Robert Miles explains how a trained model can end up with a goal that differs from the one it was trained on.

  • Alignment faking in large language modelsArticle, about 15 minutes

    An Anthropic experiment where a model behaved differently when it believed it was being trained.

Discuss: What did the alignment faking experiment show, and what does it not show about models in ordinary use?

Session 5: How worried should we be?

By the end you can: state the strongest version of the concern and of the skeptical reply.

Required

  • Why would AI want to do bad things? Instrumental convergenceVideo, about 10 minutes

    Robert Miles on why many different goals lead a capable system to seek resources and avoid being shut off.

  • A guide to understanding AI as normal technologyArticle, about 10 minutes

    Two Princeton researchers argue that AI will spread slowly, the way earlier technologies did. Read the section called A restatement of our thesis.

Optional

  • Statement on AI RiskArticle, about 2 minutes

    A one-sentence statement from the Center for AI Safety. Read the claim and ask what evidence would support it.

Discuss: Where do the two readings disagree about evidence, and where do they disagree about predictions?

Session 6: Looking inside models

By the end you can: describe one finding from interpretability research and what it lets researchers check.

Required

  • Tracing the thoughts of a large language modelArticle, about 15 minutes

    Anthropic researchers look inside a model as it plans a rhyme, does arithmetic, and makes something up.

Optional

  • Using dangerous AI, but safely?Video, about 30 minutes

    Robert Miles explains AI control: getting useful work out of a model you don't fully trust.

Discuss: What would you want to be able to see inside a model before trusting it with a decision about you?

Session 7: Rules and laws

By the end you can: name three things a government can do about AI and one drawback of each.

Required

  • The AI regulator's toolboxArticle, about 20 minutes

    A list of the things governments can do about AI. Pick three tools and read the pros and cons of each.

Optional

  • Computing power and the governance of AIArticle, about 15 minutes

    Why the chips used to train AI are one of the few things regulators can track.

Discuss: Which of these tools would have mattered for a project built in this club?

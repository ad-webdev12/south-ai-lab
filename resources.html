Start here

Three things to do before your first meeting. They take about fifteen minutes in total.

  1. Join our Classroom

    Use code selpcao in Google Classroom. Slides, notebooks, and announcements are posted there.

  2. Make two free accounts

    Kaggle for courses, datasets, and competitions, and GitHub for saving your code.

  3. Open a notebook

    Try Google Colab. It runs Python in your browser, so there's nothing to install. Bring a laptop if you have one.

Project tools

Learning planner

A self-paced path from your first line of Python to your own project. We picked a few of the best free resources for each stage, so you don't have to sort through hundreds. Check things off as you go. Your progress is saved in this browser.

1Python basicsYou can write a function, loop over a list, and make sense of an error message.
  • Kaggle Learn: PythonCourse, 5 hours

    Short lessons with exercises that run in your browser. This is the course we use in meetings.

  • CS50's Introduction to Programming with PythonCourse, weeks 0 to 4

    Harvard's free beginner course. Do the first few weeks if you want more practice than Kaggle gives you.

  • Python lessonsClub slides

    Our own deck from the March 2026 Python session.

Try it: Write a number guessing game, or a program that finds the ten most common words in a text file.

2Working with dataYou can load a CSV, clean it up, and make a chart that answers a question.
  • Kaggle Learn: PandasCourse, 4 hours

    Loading, filtering, grouping, and combining tables of data.

  • Kaggle Learn: Data VisualizationCourse, 4 hours

    Making line charts, bar charts, heatmaps, and scatter plots with seaborn.

  • NumPy: the absolute basics for beginnersArticle, 2 hours

    The official beginner's guide to arrays, which every ML library is built on.

  • Data preprocessing notebookClub notebook

    The notebook from our November 2025 session: missing values, encoding, scaling, and splitting.

  • Python Data Science HandbookBook, reference

    Free online. Look things up here when a pandas or Matplotlib question comes up.

Try it: Pick any dataset on Kaggle and make three charts that each answer one question about it.

3Your first modelsYou can train a model, test it on data it hasn't seen, and explain what the score means.
  • Kaggle Learn: Intro to Machine LearningCourse, 3 hours

    Decision trees, validation, overfitting, and random forests, ending with a competition entry.

  • Machine Learning for EverybodyVideo, 4 hours

    Kylie Ying explains and codes the classic algorithms in Google Colab, one at a time.

  • MLU-ExplainInteractive, 2 hours

    Visual explanations of ideas such as train and test splits, bias and variance, and decision trees.

  • Kaggle Learn: Intermediate Machine LearningCourse, 4 hours

    Missing values, categorical columns, pipelines, cross-validation, and XGBoost. Useful for raising your Titanic score.

  • scikit-learn: Getting StartedArticle, 1 hour

    How the library we use fits models, builds pipelines, and scores results.

  • Getting started with machine learningClub slides

    Our deck from the November 2025 session.

Try it: Enter the Titanic competition on Kaggle and try to beat 78% accuracy.

4Neural networksYou can explain how a network learns, and train one in Keras.
  • 3Blue1Brown: Neural NetworksVideo, 1 hour

    Watch the first four videos: what a neural network is, how it learns, and backpropagation. Start here.

  • TensorFlow PlaygroundInteractive, 30 minutes

    Train a tiny network in your browser and watch what each neuron learns.

  • Kaggle Learn: Intro to Deep LearningCourse, 4 hours

    Build and train networks in Keras, including dropout and batch normalization.

  • MNIST demo notebookClub notebook

    The convolutional network from our December 2025 session. Run it, then change it.

  • 3Blue1Brown: Essence of Linear AlgebraVideo, 3 hours

    Vectors and matrices explained with animation. Watch when the math starts to feel like a wall.

  • The spelled-out intro to neural networks and backpropagationVideo, 2.5 hours

    Andrej Karpathy builds a neural network library from nothing. Harder, and worth it.

Try it: Run the MNIST notebook, then change one thing at a time (layers, epochs, dropout) and record what happens to accuracy.

5Computer visionYou can train an image classifier on your own photos and find where it fails.
  • Teachable MachineInteractive, 30 minutes

    Train an image, pose, or sound classifier from your webcam. No code.

  • CNN ExplainerInteractive, 30 minutes

    See what each layer of a convolutional network does to an image.

  • Kaggle Learn: Computer VisionCourse, 4 hours

    Convolutions, pooling, data augmentation, and transfer learning in Keras.

  • Keras datasetsTool

    MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10, each loadable in one line.

  • Intro to computer visionClub slides

    Our deck from the March 2026 workshop.

Try it: Take 50 photos of two or three objects at home, train a classifier on them, and find three photos that fool it.

6Language modelsYou can explain how a chatbot produces text, and use a pretrained model in your own code.
  • Intro to Large Language ModelsVideo, 1 hour

    Andrej Karpathy's general-audience talk on what LLMs are and how they're trained.

  • Transformer ExplainerInteractive, 30 minutes

    Type a sentence and watch a real GPT-2 model predict the next word, step by step.

  • The Illustrated TransformerArticle, 1 hour

    The standard visual walkthrough of attention and the transformer architecture.

  • Hugging Face LLM CourseCourse, chapters 1 to 3

    Use pretrained models for classification, summarizing, and generation in a few lines of Python.

  • Prompt Engineering GuideArticle, 3 hours

    Techniques for getting better results out of language models, with examples.

  • Intro to NLPClub slides

    Our deck from the April 2026 workshop.

  • Let's build GPTVideo, 2 hours

    Karpathy codes a small GPT from an empty file. Save this for after the neural networks stage.

Try it: Run a pretrained sentiment model from Hugging Face on 20 reviews you write yourself. Where does it get confused?

7Agents and reinforcement learningYou can train an agent to play a simple game, or build an assistant that uses a tool.
  • Hugging Face Deep RL CourseCourse, units 1 and 2

    You train a lunar lander in the first unit. Units 1 and 2 are enough to start a project.

  • GymnasiumTool

    The standard library of game environments for reinforcement learning, with tutorials.

  • Hugging Face Agents CourseCourse, unit 1

    What an AI agent is, how tool use works, and how to build a simple one.

  • Reinforcement Learning: An IntroductionBook, reference

    Sutton and Barto's textbook, free online. Chapter 1 is a readable overview. Look up the rest if you want the theory.

Try it: Train an agent to land the Lunar Lander in Gymnasium, then change the reward and see how its behavior shifts.

Build your project

You don't need to finish the whole planner first. Once you're through stage 3, pick an idea and start. You'll learn the rest as you need it.

Ideas to start from

  • Predict something about your own life from data you collect: sleep, screen time, grades, or running times.
  • A classifier that sorts photos from your phone into categories you choose.
  • A model that guesses the genre of a song from its lyrics.
  • A study assistant that answers questions about your own class notes.
  • A game-playing agent for a simple game you know well.
  • A fairness check of a model someone else in the club built.

Find data

Most projects start with a dataset. These four sources are good places to look.

  • Kaggle DatasetsTool

    The largest collection, with notebooks showing how other people used each dataset.

  • Hugging Face DatasetsTool

    Text, image, and audio datasets that load in one line of Python.

  • UCI Machine Learning RepositoryTool

    Small, clean, classic datasets. Good for a first project.

  • Keras datasetsTool

    MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and a few more, built into Keras.

Share what you made

Put your code and a demo online so other people can try your project.

  • Project README templateTemplate

    Our one-page template: the question, the data, how to run it, results, and limitations.

  • GitHub: Hello WorldArticle, 30 minutes

    Make your first repository and learn the basics of saving code online.

  • GradioTool

    Turn a Python function into a web demo with a few lines of code.

  • StreamlitTool

    Build a small data app in pure Python.

  • Hugging Face SpacesTool

    Free hosting for Gradio and Streamlit demos.

Check your model

Before you present, find out who your model works badly for, and write it down.

  • Kaggle Learn: Model CardsArticle, 20 minutes

    What a model card is and what goes in one, with examples.

  • AI ExplorablesInteractive, 1 hour

    Short interactive essays from Google on fairness, bias in datasets, and how models fail.

  • Kaggle Learn: Intro to AI EthicsCourse, 4 hours

    Short lessons with exercises on spotting bias and measuring fairness. The Model Cards lesson above is part of it.

AI safety reading group

A six-session plan for the AI, Ethics and Society group, adapted from a university AI safety course and cut down to the most readable pieces. Each session needs about an hour of reading or watching beforehand.

Session 1: Where AI is headed

How fast is AI improving, and what is driving it?

  • Task-Completion Time HorizonsArticle, 10 minutes

    METR measures how long a task AI systems can finish on their own, and how quickly that number is growing.

  • Trends in Artificial IntelligenceInteractive, 15 minutes

    Epoch AI's charts of the computing power, data, and money going into AI.

  • Can AI scaling continue through 2030?Article, 10 minutes

    Read the summary: what could limit AI progress, from power to chips to data.

Session 2: When AI does what you said, not what you meant

Why is it hard to tell a machine what you really want?

  • Specification gaming: the flip side of AI ingenuityArticle, 15 minutes

    Google DeepMind's collection of AI systems that found loopholes in their instructions. Funny, and a little worrying.

  • Aligning language models to follow instructionsArticle, 15 minutes

    How OpenAI used human feedback to make GPT-3 follow instructions, the method behind today's chatbots.

  • Playing the training gameArticle, 20 minutes

    Why a model trained on human approval might learn to look good instead of being good.

Session 3: When a model learns the wrong goal

Can a model pass every test and still want the wrong thing?

  • The other AI alignment problem: mesa-optimizersVideo, 23 minutes

    Robert Miles explains how a trained model can end up with a goal that differs from the one it was trained on.

  • Alignment faking in large language modelsArticle, 15 minutes

    An Anthropic experiment where a model behaved differently when it believed it was being trained.

  • Sycophancy to subterfugeArticle, 15 minutes

    How small habits such as flattery can generalize into a model tampering with its own reward.

Session 4: What could go wrong at scale

Why do serious researchers worry about this, and are they right?

  • Why would AI want to do bad things? Instrumental convergenceVideo, 10 minutes

    Robert Miles on why almost any goal leads a capable system to seek resources and avoid being shut off.

  • A guide to understanding AI as normal technologyArticle, 10 minutes

    The other side. Two Princeton researchers argue that AI will spread slowly, the way earlier technologies did. Read the section called A restatement of our thesis, and the one after it about AI 2027.

  • Statement on AI RiskArticle, 2 minutes

    One sentence, signed by hundreds of AI scientists and company leaders. Look at who signed.

  • We're not ready for superintelligenceVideo, 35 minutes

    A video walkthrough of the AI 2027 scenario. Come ready to argue about which parts are plausible.

Session 5: Looking inside models, and keeping them in check

How can we tell what a model is doing, and limit the damage if it misbehaves?

  • Tracing the thoughts of a large language modelArticle, 15 minutes

    Anthropic researchers look inside a model as it plans a rhyme, does arithmetic, and makes something up.

  • Using dangerous AI, but safely?Video, 30 minutes

    Robert Miles explains AI control: getting useful work out of a model you don't fully trust.

  • Agentic misalignmentArticle, 20 minutes

    Stress tests where AI agents, given a goal and a threat, chose harmful actions. It's long: read the introduction and the blackmail example, then skim the rest.

Session 6: Rules, laws, and what you can do

Who should be responsible for AI, and where could you fit in?

  • The AI regulator's toolboxArticle, 20 minutes

    A long list of the things governments can do about AI. Don't read it all. Pick three tools and read the pros and cons of each.

  • Computing power and the governance of AIArticle, 15 minutes

    Why the chips used to train AI are one of the few things regulators can track.

  • AI safety technical researchArticle, 20 minutes

    What a career in AI safety research looks like. It's long and written for adults, so read the opening summary and the section called How to enter.

Competitions and programs

Ways to keep going outside the club.

  • Kaggle: TitanicCompetition

    The standard first competition. Our teams entered it in November 2025. It never closes.

  • Kaggle: Housing PricesCompetition

    The competition that Kaggle's Intro to Machine Learning course ends with. You predict a number this time, not a category.

  • Deep-ML practice problemsPractice

    Short coding problems where you implement pieces of machine learning yourself, checked in the browser.

  • MIT Beaver Works Summer InstituteSummer program

    A summer program from MIT Lincoln Laboratory with AI, robotics, and cybersecurity courses. Applications are usually due in March, and the online prerequisites count toward admission. Past members have attended.

  • Inspirit AISummer program

    An online, project-based AI program for high school students. It charges tuition.

About the lab

SAIL has met at High School South since 2022. Sessions are taught by members, and the subjects follow a sequence: Python and data handling in the fall, classical models and a competition entry before winter, neural networks and vision after that, and a project in the spring.

Everything the lab uses is free and runs in a browser. Members have gone on to summer research programs including MIT Beaver Works, and past sessions have included visits from researchers at MIT Lincoln Laboratory and Carnegie Mellon.

How it is organized

Members join one of two tracks. The Foundations Program is the taught track, for ninth graders and for sophomores in their first year with the lab. The Applied Research Division is for juniors and seniors working in small project teams, and is open to anyone else who applies with prior work.

Both tracks attach to one of six research groups. Group assignments happen after the first term, so that the choice is made with some idea of what each group does.

Research groups

Six groups, 2026 to 2027
G-01

Natural Language Processing

How text is represented numerically, what sequence models learn from it, and how to judge output that has no single right answer.

Tokenization and text cleaning; Bag of words, TF-IDF, and what they throw away; Word and sentence embeddings
G-02

Computer Vision

Convolutional models on image data, from a first classifier trained in a browser to controlled experiments on standard datasets.

Image preprocessing, normalization, and augmentation; Convolution, pooling, and network depth; Transfer learning on small datasets
G-03

Neural Systems

What a network is doing underneath the framework call: gradients, optimizers, and the mathematics each of them needs.

The perceptron and the forward pass; Loss functions and gradient descent; Backpropagation worked through by hand
G-04

Agents and Reinforcement Learning

Systems that take actions rather than return one prediction. New for the 2026 to 2027 year.

States, actions, rewards, and discounting; Q-learning in gridworlds; Exploration against exploitation
G-05

Applied Data Science

Tabular data, feature engineering, and competition work. The usual starting point for new members.

Loading and cleaning data with pandas; Missing values, encoding, and scaling; Train and test splits, and cross-validation
G-06

AI, Ethics and Society

Fairness, documentation, and the arguments about deployment, run as work with written output rather than open discussion.

Error rates compared across groups within a dataset; What a dataset does not contain, and who that affects; Short model documentation for released work

Two ways to join

Foundations Program

Ninth graders, and sophomores joining the lab for the first time

Taught sessions with guided exercises. Members write Python in the first session and train a working model before winter. No prior experience is assumed and no course is required beforehand.

  • Weekly instruction following the published sequence
  • Team entry to a public competition
  • A project presented at the end of the year

Program details

Applied Research Division

Juniors and seniors, or anyone who applies with prior work

Project teams of two to four members attached to a research group. Teams choose a question, agree on what they will produce, and present the result to the lab at the end of the term.

  • A short proposal agreed before the work starts
  • Code in a repository another member can run
  • A presentation and a written record at the end of term

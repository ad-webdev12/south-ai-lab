Learning path

Seven stages from a first line of Python to your own project.

Each stage has one or two resources marked Start here. The rest are alternatives or references, so you don't need to finish them all. A stage is done when you've finished its task, and you can start a project of your own after stage 3.

Resources were chosen because they are free, widely used, and assume no more than the stage before them. Times are estimates.

Progress is saved in this browser on this device only.

1Python basicsYou can write a function, loop over a list, and make sense of an error message.
  • Start hereKaggle Learn: PythonCourse, about 5 hours

    Short lessons with exercises that run in your browser. We use this one in meetings.

  • More practiceCS50's Introduction to Programming with PythonCourse, weeks 0 to 4

    Harvard's beginner course. Pick this if you want longer problem sets than Kaggle offers.

  • ReferencePython lessonsClub slides

    Our deck from the March 2026 Python session.

2Working with dataYou can load a CSV, clean it up, and make a chart that answers a question.
  • Start hereKaggle Learn: PandasCourse, about 4 hours

    Loading, filtering, grouping, and combining tables of data.

  • More practiceKaggle Learn: Data VisualizationCourse, about 4 hours

    Line charts, bar charts, heatmaps, and scatter plots with seaborn.

  • More practiceNumPy: the absolute basics for beginnersArticle, about 2 hours

    The official beginner's guide to arrays, which the ML libraries are built on.

  • ReferenceData preprocessing notebookClub notebook

    From our November 2025 session: missing values, encoding, scaling, and splitting.

  • ReferencePython Data Science HandbookBook

    Free online. Useful when a pandas or Matplotlib question comes up.

3First modelsYou can train a model, test it on data it hasn't seen, and explain what the score means.
  • Start hereKaggle Learn: Intro to Machine LearningCourse, about 3 hours

    Decision trees, validation, overfitting, and random forests, ending with a competition entry.

  • More practiceMachine Learning for EverybodyVideo, about 4 hours

    Kylie Ying explains and codes the classic algorithms in Google Colab. Pick this if you prefer video to reading.

  • More practiceMLU-ExplainInteractive, about 2 hours

    Visual explanations of train and test splits, bias and variance, and decision trees.

  • More practiceKaggle Learn: Intermediate Machine LearningCourse, about 4 hours

    Missing values, categorical columns, pipelines, cross-validation, and XGBoost.

  • Referencescikit-learn: Getting StartedArticle, about 1 hour

    How the library fits models, builds pipelines, and scores results.

  • ReferenceGetting started with machine learningClub slides

    Our deck from the November 2025 session.

4Neural networksYou can explain how a network learns, and train one in Keras.
  • Start here3Blue1Brown: Neural NetworksVideo, about 1 hour

    Watch the first four videos: what a neural network is, how it learns, and backpropagation.

  • Start hereMNIST demo notebookClub notebook

    The convolutional network from our December 2025 session.

  • More practiceTensorFlow PlaygroundInteractive, about 30 minutes

    Train a tiny network in your browser and watch what each neuron learns.

  • More practiceKaggle Learn: Intro to Deep LearningCourse, about 4 hours

    Build and train networks in Keras, including dropout and batch normalization.

  • Reference3Blue1Brown: Essence of Linear AlgebraVideo, about 3 hours

    Vectors and matrices explained with animation.

  • More practiceThe spelled-out intro to neural networks and backpropagationVideo, about 2.5 hours

    Andrej Karpathy builds a neural network library from an empty file. Harder than the rest of this stage.

5Computer visionYou can train an image classifier on your own photos and find where it fails.
  • Start hereTeachable MachineInteractive, about 30 minutes

    Train an image, pose, or sound classifier from your webcam, with no code.

  • More practiceCNN ExplainerInteractive, about 30 minutes

    Shows what each layer of a convolutional network does to an image.

  • More practiceKaggle Learn: Computer VisionCourse, about 4 hours

    Convolutions, pooling, data augmentation, and transfer learning in Keras.

  • ReferenceKeras datasetsTool

    MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10, each loadable in one line.

  • ReferenceIntro to computer visionClub slides

    Our deck from the March 2026 workshop.

6Language modelsYou can explain how a chatbot produces text, and use a pretrained model in your own code.
  • Start hereIntro to Large Language ModelsVideo, about 1 hour

    Andrej Karpathy's general-audience talk on what LLMs are and how they're trained.

  • Start hereHugging Face LLM CourseCourse, chapters 1 to 3

    Use pretrained models for classification, summarizing, and generation in a few lines of Python.

  • More practiceTransformer ExplainerInteractive, about 30 minutes

    Type a sentence and watch a GPT-2 model predict the next word, step by step.

  • More practiceThe Illustrated TransformerArticle, about 1 hour

    A visual walkthrough of attention and the transformer architecture.

  • ReferencePrompt Engineering GuideArticle

    Techniques for getting better results out of language models, with examples.

  • ReferenceIntro to NLPClub slides

    Our deck from the April 2026 workshop.

  • More practiceLet's build GPTVideo, about 2 hours

    Karpathy codes a small GPT from an empty file. Do the neural networks stage first.

7Agents and reinforcement learningYou can train an agent to play a simple game, or build an assistant that uses a tool.
  • Start hereHugging Face Deep RL CourseCourse, units 1 and 2

    You train a lunar lander in the first unit.

  • More practiceHugging Face Agents CourseCourse, unit 1

    What an LLM-based agent is, how tool use works, and how to build a simple one.

  • ReferenceGymnasiumTool

    The standard library of game environments for reinforcement learning, with tutorials.

  • ReferenceReinforcement Learning: An IntroductionBook

    Sutton and Barto's textbook, free online. Chapter 1 is a readable overview.

Join the lab

The lab takes new members every fall and reads every application. There is no cost and no prerequisite course.

Two tracks

Foundations Program

Ninth graders, and sophomores joining the lab for the first time

This track is taught from the beginning. Members write Python in the first session, work with real data within the first month or two, and train a convolutional network before the winter.

  • Taught sessions with guided exercises and shared notebooks
  • The published sequence, from Python through language models
  • A team entry to a public competition, with the feature choices explained in the next session
  • A project at the end of the year, presented to the lab
  • A research group assignment after the first term

Expected of members: attend meetings, finish the exercise for each unit, and present once during the year.

Applied Research Division

Juniors and seniors, or anyone who applies with prior work

The division does project work. Teams of two to four attach to a research group, agree on a question and a deliverable, and present the result at the end of the term. Underclassmen with prior projects can apply directly.

  • A one-page proposal agreed before the work starts
  • A repository with instructions another member can follow
  • A progress check at each meeting
  • A presentation with questions at the end of the term
  • A short written record kept with the code, including what did not work

Expected of members: roughly two hours a week outside meetings while a project is running.

What we look for

Applications are not ranked by how much someone already knows. What matters more, for project teams and later for officer roles, is attendance and finished work.

Attendance
Meetings are every other Tuesday and the sequence builds on itself. Members who attend consistently get the project roles, and attendance is the first thing looked at during officer selection.
Finished work
A small project that works is worth more on an application than an ambitious one that was left unfinished. Include the error analysis if you have it.
A specific interest
On the form below, naming a question you want to answer helps more than naming a field. It makes the group assignment easier to get right.
Explaining things to other members
Most sessions are taught by students, so members who can explain a topic to someone a year behind them are usually the ones appointed as group leads.

Questions

Do I need to know how to code?

Not for the Foundations Program. The first unit teaches Python from the beginning, and the exercises are built so that someone with no background can finish them. The Applied Research Division expects that you can already write and debug your own Python.

I am a sophomore. Which track is mine?

If this is your first year with the lab, start in the Foundations Program. If you were here last year, or you have project work you can show, apply to the Applied Research Division.

How much time does it take?

The Foundations Program is the meeting plus about an hour between sessions. The Applied Research Division is the meeting plus roughly two hours a week while a project is running, and less between projects.

Can I pick my research group right away?

You can name a preference on the form, and it is usually honored. Group assignments happen after your first term so the choice is made with some idea of what each group actually does.

Do I need a laptop or a GPU?

No. Every session runs in a browser notebook, and Colab and Kaggle provide free GPU time for the few projects that need it.

What if I miss meetings?

Materials for every session are posted in Google Classroom. Tell an officer and catch up. Attendance matters for leadership selection, not for membership.

Can I bring a friend?

Yes. Bring them to a Tuesday session. There is no cap and no application needed to visit.

Two tracks

You sign up for Foundations, and everyone who signs up is in. You apply to the Applied Research Division with a short project idea, so an officer can help shape it and find you a team.

Foundations ProgramApplied Research Division
Who it suits Anyone new to AI or coding, in any grade. Most ninth and tenth graders start here, and so do juniors and seniors who haven't programmed before. Members who have finished Foundations or have built a project before. Mostly juniors and seniors.
What you do Learn Python, work with data, enter a Kaggle competition, and train a neural network in December. Build your own project in the spring. Build a project in a team of two to four inside a research group, and present it at the end of the semester.
Time outside meetings About an hour between meetings. About two hours a week while your project is running.

Joining after the year has started? Come to any meeting and tell an officer you're new. What we've covered so far is under Meeting materials.

Questions

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Foundations starts from the first line of Python. The Applied Research Division expects you to be able to write and fix your own code.

When do I pick a research group?

You can name one when you sign up and change it at any time. Applied Research teams work with their group from October. Foundations members join group activities in the second semester.

Do I need my own laptop?

It helps, but it isn't required. Everything runs in a browser, and Google Colab and Kaggle provide the computing power.

What if I miss a meeting?

Slides and notebooks are posted on Google Classroom, and the main ones are linked under Meeting materials.

Can I bring a friend?

Yes. Anyone can come to a meeting before signing up.
